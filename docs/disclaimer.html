Intended use

What MinbarLive is for

Making a spoken lecture understandable to listeners who do not speak the language of the speaker, with the knowledge and agreement of the people being recorded. In practice that means a mosque, an institution or an individual running it openly on their own premises, on their own hardware, with their own API key, to display subtitles for an audience that is present.

What it is not for

  • Recording people who have not been told that a recording is taking place, or who have no realistic opportunity to object.
  • Surveillance, covert monitoring, or capturing conversations the operator has no legal right to capture.
  • Any deployment where transmitting the audio to a third-party AI provider would breach the law, a contract, or a duty of confidentiality that binds the operator.
  • Presenting the output as an authoritative translation of the Quran, of hadith, or of any religious ruling.

These uses are outside what this project is built for and outside what it endorses. The software has no technical means of preventing them, which is exactly why they are stated here.

Responsibility sits with the operator

MinbarLive runs entirely on the computer of the person using it. The project operates no servers and receives no data, so it has no visibility into any installation and no ability to supervise one. Every decision that matters is made locally: which microphone is opened, which AI provider receives the audio, whether recordings are kept, and who the subtitles are shown to.

The person or organisation running the software is therefore responsible for informing those present, for obtaining any consent required, for complying with the data protection and recording law that applies to them, and for the terms of the AI provider they have chosen. The privacy policy sets out exactly what leaves the machine, so that this assessment can be made with full knowledge of it.

A note for operators in Germany

Two points deserve particular attention here, and neither is a formality. First, recording the spoken word that is not publicly spoken, without the consent of the speaker, is a criminal offence under § 201 StGB. A sermon delivered to an open congregation is usually a different case from a lesson in a closed room or a conversation before and after it, and the microphone does not distinguish between them. Second, a mosque that runs MinbarLive is the controller (Verantwortlicher) within the meaning of the GDPR, and the AI provider it sends audio to is a processor (Auftragsverarbeiter): a data processing agreement under Art. 28 GDPR is required with that provider, and OpenAI, Google, Anthropic and Deepgram each offer one. Depending on the circumstances, an entry in the record of processing activities and a data protection impact assessment may also be necessary.

This is a description of the questions that arise, not legal advice, and the people behind MinbarLive are not lawyers. An institution that intends to run the software regularly should get its own advice.

Accuracy of the output

Transcription and translation are produced by AI models. They can mishear, omit, invent, or subtly change meaning, and this risk is highest with religious content, where a small error is not a small error. Verified Quranic verse translations are inserted from a fixed reference text where a verse is recognised with high confidence, but everything else is machine output and should be treated as an aid to understanding, never as a source to rely on or to quote. The application asks every new user to acknowledge this before the first session; that acknowledgement is not a formality.
